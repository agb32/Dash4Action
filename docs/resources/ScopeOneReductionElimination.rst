Scope 1 Reduction and Elimination
=================================

As discussed previously scope 1 emissions are the carbon emissions made
by directly burning fossil fuels. To reduce carbon emissions we must
stop burning fossil fuels. Usually this involves a shift by end users to
electricity as the primary source of energy and a reliance on the
electricity market moving to carbon free generation.

Identification of Scope 1 Sources
=================================

For typical DRI facilities sources of scope 1 emissions will broadly
fall into the following categories:

- Primary Electricity Generation: typically diesel (or gas)
- Backup Electricity Generation: typically diesel (or gas)
- Heating Systems: typically gas
- Facility owned/controlled petrol/diesel vehicles

Reduction and Elimination Strategies
====================================

Primary Electricity Generation
------------------------------

The main carbon reduction strategy here is to shift electricity usage to
the Electricity Grid which has a lower carbon intensity. Where this is
not possible due to regional grid capacity constraints then mitigation
strategies may include:

- Negotiating with your local Distribution Network Operator (DNO) a
  Flexible / Curtailable Connection Agreement. Your DNO may agree to let
  you use spare capacity when regional demand is lower as long as you
  agree to limit capacity when regional demand is high. This may allow
  you to run local generators for fewer hours a year thus reducing Scope
  1 emissions.
- Consider a Battery Energy Storage System. Such a system may be sized
  for typically 1-4 hours of data centre load. This in addition to a
  Flexible Connection Agreement may remove the need to start the
  generators in a curtailment event.
- Carbon-Aware Workload Scheduling is another technique to shift peak
  DRI energy usage to times when the DNO has the capacity to supply the
  electricity rather than needing local generation.
- Where primary generation must stay online due to grid constraints then
  transitioning to low carbon alternative fuels such as
  biomethane/Renewable Natural Gas (RNG) for gas generators or
  Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil (HVO) for diesel generators may be
  possible, although RNG is expensive compared to Natural Gas while HVO
  costs a more modest premium over diesel.

| Backup Electricity Generation
| Backup generation by its very nature only runs occasionally and so
  will already only have a small scope 1 carbon footprint.

Mitigation strategies may include:

- Transitioning generators to low carbon alternative fuel such as HVO.
  HVO may have a modest premium over diesel but it has an order of
  magnitude longer tank life which for a rarely used backup generator is
  a significant advantage.
- Consider a Battery Energy Storage System. Such a system sized for 2-4
  hours of data centre load may eliminate the need to start the backup
  generator in most typical power outages[1,2]. Backup generators may
  still need to be retained for extended outages [3].
- For a new facility specifying hydrogen fuel cells using green hydrogen
  may be preferable to specifying a diesel/HVO generator.

Heating Systems
---------------

Data centres themselves generate heat so heat re-use is the main
mitigation strategy to reduce scope 1 emissions. This is discussed in a
Dash4Action case study.

Facility vehicles
-----------------

Emissions here can be mitigated by reducing travel and potentially
eliminated by migrating to an electric vehicle fleet.
